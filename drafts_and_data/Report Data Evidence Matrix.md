## Transcript-Backed Behavioral Evidence

- Search behavior was inconsistent across participants. Users alternated between global search, local search, top navigation, and brute-force scanning.
- Even when participants completed tasks, they often took indirect routes, which suggests low efficiency rather than simple lack of knowledge.
- Air quality, storm reporting, and the hourly forecast graph produced the clearest think-aloud evidence of hesitation and second-guessing.
- One participant explicitly described the site as "horrible in an emergency," which supports the argument that high-priority workflows are not surfaced well enough.

## Findings Themes To Carry Into The Draft

1. Excessive text and dense information presentation increased cognitive load.
2. The homepage prioritized national alert context over the local forecast use case most users expected.
3. Common tasks were poorly prioritized relative to niche or archival content.
4. Location-specific workflows such as air quality, storm reporting, and hourly detail were difficult to discover.
5. The interface looked dated and technically dense, which reduced confidence even when data was present.
