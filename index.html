Executive Summary

The National Weather Service is a United States government agency tasked with providing weather forecasts, hazard warnings, and other weather-related information to the public. Their official website, simply known as weather.gov, holds accurate weather data by the hour since 1912. With all this data being collected rapidly, they are also the most accurate source of weather information and forecasting in the nation. Almost all weather programs, be it the app on your smartphone or the Weather Channel, source their data from the National Weather Service.

Since weather.gov has an immense amount of information spanning numerous categories, the website is very complex to navigate. For an agency renowned with having the most accurate weather data in the country, it’s unfortunate that the average daily user finds the website cumbersome for daily use. The website overall has an outdated structure, low image and resolution quality, and requires far too much in-depth navigation to find certain information.

Our team wanted to re-design this website to make it more convenient, more organized, and most importantly, more efficient. While we still want the National Weather Service to be useful as a historical archive for researchers or scientists to closely examine data, we also wanted to make the website accessible to the average digital user, regardless of the device they have, so they can find accurate weather information they might need immediately and easily.

Introduction

The Existing Product

The National Weather Service website (weather.gov) is a public platform that provides official weather forecasts, alerts, radar maps, and safety information for locations across the United States. It is run by the government and focuses on delivering accurate, real time data rather than a polished or modern design. Users can view hourly and extended forecasts, track storms on interactive radar maps, and read detailed warnings about severe weather events like hurricanes, tornadoes, floods, or winter storms. The site also includes educational content about weather patterns, safety tips, volunteering opportunities, etc, which makes it more than just a basic forecast tool. Overall, it is built to be a reliable source of information, especially during dangerous or rapidly changing weather conditions.


Target User Demographic

The main users of weather.gov are people who want accurate, detailed, and trustworthy weather information. This includes travelers planning trips, outdoor workers like construction crews, farmers monitoring conditions for crops, and emergency planners who need to prepare for severe weather events. It is also commonly used by people in specialized fields such as aviation and marine navigation, where small changes in weather and precise weather event locations can have serious consequences. Compared to more commercial weather apps, the site tends to attract users who prioritize accuracy and detail over visual design or convenience features, since the interface can feel outdated and less user friendly.


Why We Selected This Product

Our team selected this website because of its outsized importance relative to its use within the general population. Despite the NOAA being the main weather source for other weather services, weather.gov is rarely used by the typical American. We believe that redesigning this website could massively boost its usage. Additionally the website fit will within the scope of our project; its design could use a major overhaul, but its functionality and core content were fine


Initial Hypotheses

We figured the biggest usability issues come from how cluttered and unintuitive the interface feels. There’s a lot of dense information on each page, but not much visual hierarchy, so it’s hard to quickly find what you actually need. We also thought navigation would be a problem, especially for first time users who don’t already know where things are. Another assumption we had was that the site doesn’t translate well to mobile, even though a lot of people check the weather on their phones. Overall, we expected that users might trust the data but still feel frustrated trying to access it efficiently, which could push them toward easier to use alternatives.
